### Start With the Right Structure

Employers typically scan resumes for key sections: contact information, professional summary, work experience, education, and skills. A well-structured resume guides the reader through your qualifications naturally.

**Tip:** Keep it concise, clean, and results-driven.

### Focus on Achievements, Not Just Duties

Rather than listing what you were responsible for, highlight what you accomplished. Use quantifiable metrics whenever possible.

**Example:**
- Bad: "Responsible for managing social media accounts"
- Good: "Increased social media engagement by 250% over 6 months, driving 1,500+ new leads"

### Tailor Your Resume to Each Position

Generic resumes rarely land interviews. Review the job description and mirror the language, emphasizing relevant skills and experiences.

### Use Action Verbs

Start bullet points with strong action verbs: "Led," "Implemented," "Optimized," "Designed," "Launched." This makes your experience more dynamic and impactful.

### Keep It Professional

Use a clean, modern font (like Arial or Calibri), consistent formatting, and plenty of white space. Save as a PDF to preserve formatting across devices.

### Proofread Thoroughly

Typos and grammatical errors can immediately disqualify you. Review your resume multiple times and consider having someone else look it over.
